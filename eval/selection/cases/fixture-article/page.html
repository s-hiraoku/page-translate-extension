How tides work

By Jane Doe, staff writer · March 3, 2026 · 6 min read
Skip to main content now

Tides are the regular rise and fall of sea level, caused by the gravitational pull of the Moon and the Sun acting on the rotating Earth, and they shape coastlines everywhere.

The role of the Moon

The Moon's gravity pulls on the ocean nearest to it, creating a bulge of water. A second bulge forms on the opposite side, because the Earth itself is pulled toward the Moon more strongly than the distant water.

As the Earth rotates through these bulges, most coastlines experience two high tides and two low tides every lunar day, which lasts about twenty-four hours and fifty minutes.

  • Spring tides happen when the Sun and Moon align, producing larger ranges.
  • Neap tides happen when they are at right angles, producing smaller ranges.
